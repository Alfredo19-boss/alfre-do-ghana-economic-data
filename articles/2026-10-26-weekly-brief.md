# Ghana's economy, week to 26 October 2026

_Public debt is running at about GH¢747.3bn, inflation stands at 5.2% for Sep 2026, and the policy rate is 14.0%. This briefing is assembled from the published figures on the dashboard, each with the date it belongs to._

## The debt

Ghana's public debt is estimated at **GH¢747.3bn** this morning. That is not a measurement: the Bank of Ghana's most recent published total is **GH¢730.3bn** at 31 August 2026, and the counter carries it forward at the pace debt grew between 28 February 2026 and 31 August 2026 — **GH¢3,537 a second**, or about GH¢305.6 million a day.

At that level, each of Ghana's roughly 34.4 million people carries about **GH¢21,724** of public debt. Debt-to-GDP was reported at 45.7% at the latest reading, against a peak of 61.8% at end-2024.

## Prices and interest rates

Inflation was **5.2%** in Sep 2026 — up from 5.0% in Aug 2026. The Bank of Ghana's policy rate is **14.0%** (24 Sep 2026), which leaves a real policy rate of about **8.8 percentage points** once inflation is taken out.

The 91-day Treasury bill last cleared at **4.61%** (26 Oct 2026) — unchanged from Oct 2026. Petrol is **GH¢16.09** a litre (16–31 Oct 2026).

## The cedi, reserves and commodities

The cedi traded at **GH¢11.6500** to the dollar on 23 October 2026, weaker than the 10.4500 recorded at End-2025 — a move of 11.5%.

Gross reserves stood at **US$12.1bn** (22 Sep 2026). Gold is trading at **US$4,408** an ounce and cocoa at **US$5,989** a tonne, as at 25 October 2026.

## Trade

Exports reached **US$22.4bn** in Jan–Aug 2026, of which gold alone was **US$14.9bn** — 66% of everything the country sold abroad. Against imports of about **US$13.6bn**, that leaves a trade surplus of **US$8.9bn**.

That concentration cuts both ways: the same gold price that has carried the surplus and steadied the cedi is a single market Ghana does not control.

## The budget, so far

The 2026 budget approved **GH¢268.1bn** of revenue and grants against **GH¢302.5bn** of spending. Spread evenly, 82% of the year has passed, which puts revenue at about **GH¢219.3bn** and spending at **GH¢247.4bn** — an even-pace estimate, not collection to date.

Interest on debt is budgeted at **GH¢57.7bn** for the year, which is **25.8%** of tax revenue: roughly 22 pesewas of every cedi the state collects.
